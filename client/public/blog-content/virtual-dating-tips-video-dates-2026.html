What Is Virtual Dating?

Virtual dating means getting to know someone over video before — sometimes instead of — meeting them in person. It's not a replacement for real dates so much as a screening step: a live video call gives you a much better read on someone than a week of text messages, without either of you having to commit to a full evening out.

Why Video-First Dating Took Off

📊 What Match's Singles in America Survey Found

  • 27% of singles overall had a video date before meeting in person — rising to 51% of Gen Z and 45% of Millennials.
  • 71% of those who did said it directly helped them decide whether to meet up.
  • 78% of singles who video dated said they felt real romantic chemistry through the call.
  • 81% of people who tried a video date reported little to no awkwardness, despite 58% expecting it to be awkward beforehand.

Source: Fast Company, reporting on Match's Singles in America survey (conducted with The Kinsey Institute).

The appeal is straightforward: a video call surfaces things text simply can't — tone of voice, timing, genuine laughter, how someone reacts in the moment. It's also a lower-stakes commitment than a full date, which is part of why the fear of awkwardness turned out to be so much bigger than the actual experience of it.

The Case for Text Before Video

Jumping straight to video with a total stranger is its own kind of pressure — you're essentially cold-opening a conversation with your camera on. The stronger sequence, and the one worth defaulting to, is text first, video second: get a feel for how someone writes and thinks before you're both staring at a live feed of each other.

By the time a video call is even an option, you already know the conversation is worth having on camera.

Tips for a Good Virtual Date

  1. Keep the first call short. Fifteen to twenty minutes is plenty to gauge chemistry — treat it as a screening step, not the whole date.
  2. Actually look at the camera, not the screen. Small thing, but it reads as eye contact and makes the conversation feel less like a video call and more like talking to a person.
  3. Pick a moment you're not rushed. A call squeezed between other things comes across, even unintentionally.
  4. Let it be a little awkward. Given that most people who tried it reported little to no awkwardness despite expecting plenty, the anticipation is usually worse than the reality — a stray silence or a bad joke isn't a sign it's going badly.
  5. Use it as a genuine decision point, not a formality. If the chemistry clearly isn't there on video, it's fine to end the connection there rather than pushing toward an in-person date anyway.

Staying Safe on a Video Date

📊 Why the Video Step Matters

Romance scams cost U.S. consumers $1.14 billion in 2023 across more than 64,000 reports, the highest median loss of any imposter-scam category the FTC tracks. A video call isn't a guarantee — scammers with access to real footage of someone can and do use it to extend a con — but someone unwilling to ever appear on a live call, after weeks of texting, is one of the more consistent warning signs across these cases.

Source: Federal Trade Commission, "Love stinks — when a scammer is involved" (February 2024).

  • Use the app's built-in calling rather than moving to a personal number or another app before you've built some trust.
  • A video call is a genuinely useful check, but not a foolproof one — ask for something spontaneous mid-call (wave a hand, say a specific word) rather than treating the call itself as proof.
  • If someone repeatedly avoids or delays a video call after weeks of texting, treat that as a real signal, not a coincidence.
  • Still meet the first in-person date in public, even after a great video call.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is virtual dating the same as online dating?

Not quite — online dating describes meeting someone through an app or site in general. Virtual dating specifically refers to the video-call portion, usually as a step between messaging and meeting in person.

How long should a first video date last?

Fifteen to twenty minutes is generally enough to get a real read on someone. Treat it as a screening step rather than a full date, and let it naturally run longer only if it's clearly going well.

Is it normal to feel awkward on a video date?

It's common to expect awkwardness beforehand, but survey data suggests most people who actually try it report little to no awkwardness in practice — the anticipation tends to be worse than the real thing.

Should you video call before or after texting?

Text first is generally the stronger sequence — it lets you gauge how someone communicates before adding the pressure of a live camera, and gives the eventual video call a much better foundation.
