Why people look for a Monkey alternative

Monkey popularized fast, face-to-face video matching for a younger crowd, and that format is genuinely fun for a lot of people. But it comes with friction that sends plenty of users looking elsewhere:

  • It's app-only. You install it from the App Store or Google Play — there's no "just open it in a browser on my laptop" option.
  • It wants an account. Signing up and handing over details is the opposite of anonymous, and it's a hard stop if you only wanted a quick chat.
  • The format is rushed. Short, timed video calls are built for speed, not for an actual conversation — if you want to slow down or start with text, it fights you.
  • Age and availability wobble. Like most teen-skewing video apps, its store availability and age rating have shifted over time, so the version you remember may not be the one you find.

If none of that bothers you, Monkey is fine. If one or more of them is exactly why you're here, keep reading.

What makes a good Monkey alternative

Before the list, here's the checklist we'd use ourselves. A strong Monkey alternative should:

  • Work in a browser — on a phone and a desktop, with no download.
  • Skip the account — or at least let you start chatting before you sign up.
  • Match you instantly and let you skip to the next person in one tap.
  • Offer text and video, so you choose the pace instead of being forced onto camera.
  • Take safety seriously — moderation, one-tap reporting, and no permanent message logs.

2. OmeTV — established and moderated

One of the better-known Omegle-style sites, OmeTV offers random video chat on both web and app with active moderation. It leans video-first and shows ads, but it's a dependable, widely used option.

3. Chatroulette — the original random cam

The site that started the random-webcam category is still running and works in the browser. It's video-centric with its own moderation layer; expect a rawer, less predictable experience than a polished app.

4. Emerald Chat — interest tags, Omegle-style

Emerald Chat pairs you by interest tags and offers both text and video. It's a popular Omegle replacement and turns up constantly in "sites like Omegle" roundups.

5. Chatspin — video chat with filters

Chatspin offers random video chat with face filters and gender/location filters (some behind a paid tier). It's available on web and as an app, and sits closer to Monkey's polished, feature-heavy feel.

6. Azar — closest to Monkey's format (but still an app)

If it's specifically the fast 1:1 video-matching format you liked about Monkey, Azar is the nearest match. Fair warning: like Monkey, it's an app-based product with sign-up — so it solves the "format" complaint but not the "no download, no account" one.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a Monkey alternative with no download?

Yes. Chatrio runs entirely in your web browser on phone or desktop — there's nothing to install and no account to create. OmeTV and Chatroulette also work in the browser, though they're video-first and show ads.

What's the best free Monkey app alternative?

For a free, no-sign-up option you can use instantly, Chatrio is our pick because it works in the browser and offers both text and one-tap video. If you specifically want Monkey's fast 1:1 video format, Azar or Chatspin are closer, but they're apps that require an account.

Is Monkey still available?

Monkey is still distributed as a mobile app, but — like many teen-oriented video-chat apps — its store availability and age rating have changed over time and vary by region. If you can't find the version you remember, a browser-based alternative avoids the app-store issue entirely.

Are Monkey alternatives safe?

Safety depends on the platform and on you. Look for active moderation, one-tap reporting, and no stored message logs, and keep the basics automatic: don't share identifying details, stay on-platform, never send money, and leave any chat that feels off. Our piece on why Omegle shut down explains why moderation matters so much in this category.

Can I use a Monkey alternative on a desktop or laptop?

Monkey itself is mobile-only, so to chat on a computer you'll need a web-based alternative. Chatrio, Chatroulette, and OmeTV all run in a desktop browser.
