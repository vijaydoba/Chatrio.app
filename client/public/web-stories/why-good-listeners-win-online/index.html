Everyone thinks they listen. Almost nobody does.

Most people are waiting to respond, not actually listening. That gap — genuine attention — is so rare that people notice it instantly when you do it.

Reference what they said 10 messages ago.

Coming back to something someone said earlier — 'wait, going back to what you said about your sister' — signals that you actually absorbed it. That's deeply flattering and rare.

Ask follow-ups that couldn't exist without listening.

A generic question could come from anyone. A question that builds specifically on what they just shared proves you were fully there. That's the difference.

Listening isn't passive.

Great listening in text means short acknowledgments ('wait, seriously?', 'that's actually wild'), real reactions, and questions that prove you cared about the answer.

People remember who made them feel heard.

Not who said the cleverest thing. Not who had the best story. Who made them feel genuinely, completely heard. Be that person. It's not hard — it just requires actually paying attention.
