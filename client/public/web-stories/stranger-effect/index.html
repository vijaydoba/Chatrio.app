Psychologists have a name for it: the stranger on a train effect.

You end up next to someone you'll never see again — and you tell them things you've never told your closest friends. Why? Because they have no stake in your life. No history. No power to judge you in any way that matters.

🛡️

Permanence makes us guarded.

With people who are permanent fixtures in our lives, honesty carries risk. Say the wrong thing and it lives in the relationship forever. With a stranger, the conversation ends and so does any consequence.

🌐

The internet amplified this effect enormously.

Anonymous online chat platforms created the stranger-on-a-train effect at global scale. Billions of conversations that wouldn't have happened in any other context — honest, vulnerable, surprising.

📊

The effect is real and measurable.

Studies on online disclosure consistently show people share more personal information with anonymous strangers than with known contacts. The privacy of anonymity lowers the cost of honesty to almost zero.
