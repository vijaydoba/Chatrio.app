The overthinking loop is the conversation killer.

Draft, delete, redraft. By the time you've perfected the message, the moment's gone — and so is the energy that made the conversation worth having.

First draft, send.

Not always — but more often than you do. The message you'd send without thinking for 90 seconds is usually the authentic one. That's the one that lands.

They're not analyzing your every word.

The elaborate subtext you're worried about? They're not picking it apart. They're just reading a message and deciding how they feel about it. Keep it human.

Imperfect and present beats perfect and late.

A slightly clumsy message sent in the moment connects. A polished message sent after the conversation has cooled doesn't. Speed over polish, every time.

The goal is connection, not performance.

You're not writing an essay. You're talking to a person. Let it be messy, spontaneous, real. That's what connection actually requires.
