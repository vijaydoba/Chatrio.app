What Is Kitten Fishing? Signs of a Slightly Misleading Profile (2026)

What Is Kitten Fishing?

Kitten fishing is presenting a mildly misleading version of yourself on a dating profile — older photos, a shaved-off age, an exaggerated job title, or interests listed more out of aspiration than actual practice. It's a small-scale, low-stakes cousin of catfishing: nobody's identity is fake, just the edges are softened in whatever direction feels more appealing.

Most people have done a version of it, even in small ways. What separates ordinary profile flattery from kitten fishing is degree — a slightly better lighting choice is normal; a five-year-old photo that no longer resembles you in person is the kind of gap that causes a real, if minor, letdown when someone actually meets you.

Kitten Fishing vs Catfishing

Common Forms It Takes

  • Photos that are several years, hairstyles, or body changes out of date, chosen because they're the most flattering ones available rather than the most current.
  • A job title rounded up — "consultant" instead of a more specific, less impressive-sounding role.
  • An age listed a year or two younger than reality, often to stay inside a filtering range other people search by.
  • Interests and hobbies listed as active that are really things someone did once, or would like to do more of.
  • Heavy filters or flattering angles used consistently, rather than as one photo among several more accurate ones.

Why People Do It

📖 A Small Edit to Get Past the First Filter

Most kitten fishing comes from insecurity about clearing that first, brutal filtering pass — the swipe or the scroll where a profile gets judged in seconds. The logic is that a slightly better-looking, slightly younger, slightly more impressive version gets further, and the truth can catch up once there's already some connection. It's a short-term fix for a long-term problem, since the gap still has to be closed eventually, usually on a first date.

What to Do If You Notice It

  1. Ask for a recent, unfiltered photo before meeting, framed casually rather than as an accusation.
  2. Suggest a quick video call first. It resolves most of the ambiguity in about thirty seconds.
  3. Decide how much the specific gap actually matters to you. A photo that's a year old is different from one that's a decade old.
  4. Give a small amount of grace on a first meeting. Mild profile flattery is common enough that it's rarely worth ending things over on its own.
  5. If the gap is large or the pattern repeats with excuses attached, treat it as a more serious signal rather than an isolated slip.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is kitten fishing considered lying?

It's a mild, usually low-stakes form of it — more self-flattery than deception, though the person on the receiving end can still reasonably feel misled.

How is it different from just having a good photo?

A good photo of the current you is normal. Kitten fishing is a photo or detail that no longer reflects reality in a way that would surprise someone meeting you.

Should I call someone out for kitten fishing?

A light, direct comment usually works better than a confrontation — most kitten fishing isn't malicious, and most people will own up to it if asked plainly.

Is video chat a reliable way to avoid it?

Yes — it's one of the fastest ways to confirm someone matches their profile before investing more time, and it filters out anything more serious than a flattering photo, too.
