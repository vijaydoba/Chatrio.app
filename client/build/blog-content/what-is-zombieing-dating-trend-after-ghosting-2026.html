What Is Zombieing?

Zombieing is when someone who ghosted you — vanished without explanation — resurfaces out of nowhere weeks or months later with a casual message, as if the silence never happened. No apology, no acknowledgment of the disappearance, just a "hey stranger" or a like on an old photo, like a contact rising back from the dead.

The name is a natural extension of ghosting: the person didn't actually leave your life for good, they just went dormant and came back without doing any of the work that a real return would usually require.

Where the Term Comes From

A Natural Sequel to "Ghosting"

"Zombieing" is widely credited to writer Alexandra Whittaker, who coined it in a 2018 dating-trends piece to describe exactly this pattern — an ex-ghost reanimating in your inbox. It caught on quickly because the metaphor was so precise: the relationship wasn't dead, it was undead, capable of shambling back at any moment with no warning.

Why Zombieing Happens

It's rarely a grand plan. More often, the person who ghosted feels a passing pull of curiosity, boredom, or loneliness, and messaging costs them nothing — they've already avoided the hard conversation once, so there's no new discomfort in reaching out casually now. It keeps you as a low-effort option without requiring them to account for the silence in between.

How to Handle Being Zombied

  1. Notice what's missing from the message. A casual "hey" with no acknowledgment of the disappearance is information on its own.
  2. You're not obligated to respond at all. Silence was their choice; ignoring the resurfacing can be entirely fair too.
  3. If you do reply, name the gap directly. "You disappeared for two months — what happened?" puts the burden of explanation back where it belongs.
  4. Watch how they respond to being asked. Real accountability sounds different from a shrug or a joke about it.
  5. Don't let curiosity substitute for an actual answer. Wanting to know why isn't the same as owing them a second chance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is zombieing the same as a genuine reconciliation attempt?

Not by default. A genuine attempt usually opens by acknowledging the silence; zombieing typically skips that step entirely and just resumes as if nothing happened.

Should I ever respond to being zombied?

It's entirely your call. Responding to ask what happened can bring real closure; ignoring it is just as valid if you'd rather not reopen it.

Why do people zombie instead of just staying gone?

Usually low-effort curiosity or loneliness, not a change of heart about how they treated you the first time.

Is zombieing more common after a long silence or a short one?

It applies at any gap length, but the longer and more unexplained the silence, the more the casual reappearance tends to stand out.
