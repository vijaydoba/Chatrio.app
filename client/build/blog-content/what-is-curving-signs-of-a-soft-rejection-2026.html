What Is Curving?

Curving is deflecting a direct question or request — usually a plan, an invitation, or a "so are we doing this" moment — without ever giving a real yes or no. Instead of a clear answer, you get a joke, a vague "maybe," a change of subject, or a reply that technically responds to the message while dodging the actual question inside it.

It's a soft rejection dressed up as ambiguity. The person curving isn't necessarily lying about being busy or unsure — they're avoiding the discomfort of a direct no, which leaves the other person doing the work of reading between the lines instead of getting a straight answer.

How It Shows Up in Chat

  • Responding to "want to hang out this weekend?" with a joke instead of a yes, no, or a specific alternative.
  • Answering a different part of the message than the actual question you asked, as if the invitation part didn't register.
  • A string of "maybe," "we'll see," or "let's play it by ear" that never resolves into an actual plan, no matter how many times it comes up.
  • Enthusiastic replies to everything except the specific ask, keeping the conversation warm while never committing to anything concrete.
  • Going quiet right after a direct question, then resuming normal conversation later as if it was never asked.

Why People Curve Instead of Saying No

Saying no directly risks an uncomfortable reaction, a conversation about why, or simply feeling unkind. Curving avoids all of that in the short term — it lets the other person eventually conclude the answer on their own, without the curver ever having to say the word. It's lower-conflict for the person doing it and considerably more confusing for the person on the receiving end, which is exactly the tradeoff that makes it appealing to avoid a direct conversation.

Curving vs Someone Who's Genuinely Busy

Genuinely busyCurving
PatternOne-off, with a real reason offeredRepeats across multiple attempts
Follow-throughProposes another specific timeNever lands on an actual plan
DirectnessSays plainly they can't make itDeflects rather than answering at all

If the pattern is sustained rather than a single deflection, it may be closer to what we cover in our guide to breadcrumbing — curving is often the specific mechanism a breadcrumber uses to keep a direct question from ever landing.

How to Respond

  1. Ask the question again, more specifically. Replacing "want to hang out sometime?" with a concrete day and time removes some of the room to deflect.
  2. Notice the pattern across more than one attempt. A single dodge might be nothing; a repeated one is an answer.
  3. Take the deflection as information, not a puzzle to solve. Someone who wants to make plans with you generally does, plainly.
  4. Stop initiating and see what happens. If the plan only ever exists when you push for it, that tells you what you need to know.
  5. Look for people who answer directly instead. Chatrio puts you in a real-time conversation where there's no space for a vague "maybe" to hide in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is curving the same as ghosting?

No — ghosting is a full stop in communication, while curving keeps the conversation going in every direction except the one you actually asked about.

Should I call out curving directly?

You can ask for a straight answer once or twice, but if the deflection keeps happening after that, the pattern itself has already answered the question.

Is curving always intentional?

Usually it's at least a little deliberate, even if the person doing it wouldn't describe it that way — actively avoiding a direct question takes more effort than just answering it.

How is curving different from someone being genuinely indecisive?

Indecision usually still engages honestly with the question, even if the answer is unclear. Curving avoids engaging with the question at all.
