There is a reason "So, what do you do?" almost never leads anywhere. Small talk is designed to be safe, which means it is also designed to reveal almost nothing. If you want to actually know someone — a new match, a coworker, a date, a stranger you just met online — you need questions that quietly give both of you permission to say something true.

This is a list of 115 of them, arranged from easiest to deepest. You will not use all 115 in one sitting, and you shouldn't. Think of it as a menu you can reach for whenever a conversation stalls in the shallow end.

Why Questions Beat Small Talk

In a 2010 study published in Psychological Science, psychologist Matthias Mehl and colleagues recorded snippets of people's everyday conversations for four days. The finding was striking: the happiest people in the study had roughly twice as many substantive conversations and about a third less small talk than the least happy people. Depth, it turns out, is not just pleasant — it's linked to how connected and content we feel.

A good question is the fastest shortcut to depth, because it hands the other person an easy on-ramp. Instead of waiting for them to volunteer something real, you offer them a door and let them choose to walk through it.

📊 The One Rule That Makes This Work

  • Never ask a question you're not willing to answer yourself — take turns.
  • Climb the ladder in order; jumping straight to deep questions feels like an interrogation.
  • Follow good tangents. The list is a map, not a script.
  • "Pass" is always allowed. Consent applies to conversation too.

The Getting-to-Know-You Ladder

Closeness grows through what researchers call escalating, reciprocal self-disclosure — you share a little, they share a little more, and the trust compounds. That's the same principle behind the famous 36 questions that make strangers feel close. The sets below are ordered so you can climb naturally: start light, and only go deeper once it feels earned.

25 Easy Icebreakers

Low-stakes and genuinely fun to answer. Perfect for the first few minutes.

  1. What's something small that made you smile this week?
  2. Are you more of a morning person or a night owl?
  3. What's the last thing you got weirdly excited about?
  4. Coffee, tea, or something else entirely?
  5. What show are you embarrassed to admit you've rewatched?
  6. If you had a completely free day tomorrow, what would you do?
  7. What's your comfort food when nothing else will do?
  8. Do you have a song stuck in your head right now?
  9. What's the best thing you've eaten recently?
  10. Beach trip or mountain trip?
  11. What hobby would you pick up if time and money weren't an issue?
  12. What's your go-to way to relax after a long day?
  13. Are you a planner or a "figure it out as you go" person?
  14. What's a small thing you're really good at?
  15. What's the last photo you took on your phone?
  16. Do you collect anything?
  17. What's your ideal weekend look like?
  18. Sweet or savory?
  19. What's a movie you can quote from memory?
  20. What's your favorite way to waste time?
  21. City, suburbs, or countryside?
  22. What's something you're looking forward to?
  23. What's the best advice you've gotten lately?
  24. Do you have a favorite season, and why?
  25. What's a small luxury you can't live without?

25 Getting-to-Know-You Questions

A step up — these invite stories, opinions, and a bit of personality.

  1. What's something you've changed your mind about in the last few years?
  2. Who's had the biggest influence on who you are today?
  3. What did you want to be when you were a kid?
  4. What's a place that feels like home to you?
  5. What's the most spontaneous thing you've ever done?

  7. What's a skill you'd love to master?
  8. What's the best trip you've ever taken?
  9. What kind of person were you in school?
  10. What's something people usually get wrong about you?
  11. What's a book, film, or song that changed how you see things?
  12. What does a good day look like for you now versus five years ago?
  13. What's the bravest thing you've done?
  14. What are you most proud of that you rarely talk about?
  15. What's a tradition you love — family or your own?
  16. What's something you're working on about yourself?
  17. Who do you go to when things get hard?
  18. What's a compliment you'll never forget?
  19. What's your relationship with your hometown like?
  20. What's something that instantly puts you in a good mood?
  21. What's a risk that paid off for you?
  22. What's a risk that didn't — and what did you learn?
  23. What's a cause or issue you actually care about?
  24. How do you like to be supported when you're stressed?
  25. What's something you'd do more of if you weren't afraid?

20 Values & Belief Questions

These reveal how someone actually sees the world. Ask once there's some warmth.

  1. What does a life well lived look like to you?
  2. What's a value you refuse to compromise on?
  3. Do you think people fundamentally change? Why or why not?
  4. What does success mean to you now?
  5. What's something society accepts that you quietly disagree with?
  6. How do you decide whether to trust someone?
  7. What role does family play in your life?
  8. What do you think you owe the people you love?
  9. When was the last time you changed your mind about something important?
  10. What's a belief you were raised with that you've kept — or dropped?
  11. What does forgiveness mean to you?
  12. What's the difference between a good life and a happy one?
  13. How do you want to be remembered?
  14. What's something you think everyone should experience at least once?
  15. What matters more to you: freedom or security?
  16. How do you handle being wrong?
  17. What's a line you won't cross, even under pressure?
  18. What do you think makes a relationship last?
  19. What's something you're still figuring out about yourself?
  20. What would you tell your younger self if you had 30 seconds?

20 Deep, Revealing Questions

The top of the ladder. Only climb here if the conversation has earned it — and answer them yourself, too.

  1. What's something you're afraid to want?
  2. When do you feel most like yourself?
  3. What's a loss that shaped who you are?
  4. What's something you've never told the people closest to you?
  5. What are you hardest on yourself about?
  6. What does love feel like to you when it's real?
  7. What's a moment you'd relive if you could?
  8. What's the loneliest you've ever felt, and what got you through?
  9. What do you need more of in your life right now?
  10. What's a dream you've quietly let go of?
  11. When did you last feel truly proud of yourself?
  12. What's something you're grieving that isn't a person?
  13. What part of your life would surprise the people who think they know you?
  14. What's a fear that's been running quietly in the background?
  15. What's the kindest thing someone has done for you?
  16. What do you wish you could ask for more easily?
  17. What's something you've forgiven yourself for — or are trying to?
  18. What makes you feel safe with another person?
  19. What would "enough" look like for you?
  20. If nothing were off the table, what would you change about your life this year?

15 Playful & Hypothetical Questions

Depth is great, but connection also lives in laughter. Break tension with these.

  1. If you could have dinner with anyone, living or dead, who would it be?
  2. What fictional world would you actually want to live in?
  3. If you had to eat one meal for the rest of your life, what is it?
  4. What's the most useless talent you have?
  5. If you could instantly master one skill, what would it be?
  6. What would the title of your autobiography be?
  7. If you won the lottery tomorrow, what's the first ridiculous thing you'd buy?
  8. What animal would you be, and why?
  9. What's a conspiracy theory you find kind of fun (even if you don't believe it)?
  10. If you could time-travel to one era, when would you go?
  11. What's your most controversial food opinion?
  12. What superpower would you actually use in everyday life?
  13. If your life had a theme song, what would it be?
  14. What would you do with an extra hour every day?
  15. If you could send one message to the whole world, what would it say?

  10. Talk it out or sleep on it?

Frequently Asked Questions

How many questions should I actually ask?

Fewer than you'd think. Two or three good questions with real follow-ups beat twenty rapid-fire ones. The goal is a conversation, not a survey. Ask, listen, share your own answer, and let it wander.

What if the other person gives short answers?

Drop back down the ladder. Short answers usually mean you climbed too fast. Return to icebreakers, share more of your own answers to lower the stakes, and give them time to warm up.

Are these good for a first date or just online chat?

Both. The ladder works anywhere two people are getting to know each other — a date, a new friendship, or a stranger you matched with online. Online, the slight anonymity often makes people open up even faster.

The Bottom Line

Knowing someone isn't a single dramatic conversation — it's a series of small, returned honesties. Start light, take real turns, and let the depth build on its own. Keep this list handy, and you'll never again watch a promising conversation die at "So, what do you do?"
