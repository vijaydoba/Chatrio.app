What Is Orbiting?

Orbiting is when someone cuts off direct communication — no replies, no calls, nothing — but keeps passively engaging with you on social media: watching your stories, liking old posts, staying visibly present without ever actually talking to you again. You're not fully cut off, and you're not really connected either. You're just being kept in their orbit — close enough to notice, far enough to never talk to.

That's close to the original explanation from the person who coined the term. Writer Anna Iovine described watching a man she'd been on two dates with stop replying to texts entirely — while remaining consistently one of the first people to view her Instagram stories. The behavior didn't fit "ghosting," since he clearly hadn't disappeared. It needed its own name.

Where the Term Came From

📖 A Word Coined for a Behavior That Already Existed

Iovine coined "orbiting" while pitching a trend piece, describing the person doing it as keeping their target "in their orbit" — visible, monitored, but never engaged with directly. The term caught on quickly enough that it was shortlisted for a major dictionary's word of the year, and it's since been widely covered as one of the more common ways modern dating quietly ends without ever actually ending.

Source: Fortune, "'Orbiting' is the latest dating nightmare fueling Gen Z's disillusionment with finding love on the apps".

Why People Orbit Instead of Just Leaving

Orbiting rarely comes from a coordinated plan — it's usually the path of least resistance. Fully unfollowing or blocking someone takes a deliberate action and can feel unnecessarily harsh over a few dates. Replying to a message they don't want to send takes emotional effort. Watching a story costs nothing and requires no decision at all. The result is a low-effort, low-accountability way to keep someone as a backup option, monitor their life, or simply avoid the small discomfort of a clean ending — without the discomfort of one.

It's rarely about you specifically as much as it's about the orbiter avoiding an uncomfortable conversation. That doesn't make it less confusing to be on the receiving end of, especially when the story views keep arriving right on schedule.

What to Do When You're Being Orbited

  1. Don't read the views as interest. A story view costs nothing and isn't a substitute for the effort of an actual message.
  2. Mute or unfollow if the visibility is keeping you stuck. You don't owe an explanation, and you're allowed to remove the reminder.
  3. Resist the urge to post "for" them. Orbiting can pull both people into a strange, silent performance for an audience of one — it's worth noticing if you've started doing it too.
  4. Treat the silence as your actual answer. Someone who wanted to talk to you would talk to you; the orbit itself is the information.
  5. Redirect the energy toward people who are actually available. Chatrio is a straightforward place to start a real, two-way conversation instead of waiting on a one-way one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is orbiting always intentional?

Not necessarily calculated, but it is a choice, even a passive one — someone actively views your content while choosing not to reply. Intent aside, the healthy response is the same: don't wait on it.

Should I confront someone who's orbiting me?

You can, but expect it to rarely produce the outcome you want. A direct question ("are we talking again or not?") at least forces clarity, even if the honest answer is one you don't love.

Is orbiting worse than ghosting?

Many people find it harder to move on from, since ghosting has a clear end point and orbiting doesn't — the ongoing visibility can keep hope alive long after the connection has effectively ended.

What's the difference between orbiting and just following someone casually?

Context. Orbiting specifically follows a period of direct communication that abruptly stopped, while the passive social presence continues — it's the contrast between the two that defines the pattern.
