A nearby chat app has to answer a difficult question: how can it introduce you to people close by without making you easy to locate? Circles is Chatrio's answer. It is an anonymous nearby chat experience that uses approximate location, a nickname, and permission-based introductions instead of a public map, a real-name profile, or an open inbox.

You can open Circles in your browser, choose how you appear, see people and group rooms in your broad area, and start with one respectful intro. Here is the full walkthrough before you try it.

What Is the Circles App?

Circles is a free local chat app inside Chatrio. Its purpose is simple: help adults discover and talk with people nearby while revealing as little personal information as possible. It is not a dating feed, a public social profile, or a map of everyone around you. There is no swiping and no requirement to upload a face photo.

The experience has two paths. The Nearby path lets you send a single intro request to one person. The Groups path lets you discover or create a room connected to your approximate area. Both paths use the same anonymous identity and the same block-and-report controls.

Anonymous does not mean invisible to the people you choose to talk with. They can still see the nickname and avatar you selected, the messages you send, and a rough sense that you are nearby. It means your ordinary identity is not the price of starting a conversation.

How Circles Works, Step by Step

  1. Choose a nickname. Use something that feels human but does not expose your full name, workplace, school, or social handle.
  2. Pick your look. A generated avatar gives the nearby list enough personality without asking for a real photo.
  3. Confirm you are 18 or older. Nearby discovery, location sharing, and messaging are adult-only.
  4. Allow location while you use the feature. Circles converts the location into a broader area for matching; other users are not shown your coordinates.
  5. Browse people or groups nearby. Distance appears as a broad label rather than a house-level map pin.
  6. Send one intro. The other person decides whether a real chat opens.

One intro means one decision

An intro request is not an unlimited direct-message channel. If the recipient accepts, the conversation opens. If they decline, the sender cannot keep trying. That makes the first message more deliberate and gives the recipient a clean way to say no.

Nearby Groups and One-to-One Chat

One-to-one chat is useful when a particular profile catches your attention, but it can feel intense when neither person knows what to say. Local group rooms create an easier entry point. A room can be about a coffee walk, a book, weekend plans, language practice, a hobby, or simply conversation at a certain time of day.

Group chat also gives you context before a private introduction. You can see how someone participates in a shared conversation, whether they respect other people, and whether you actually have something to discuss. The Safety Controls That Matter

  • Approximate location: local discovery works without placing your exact position on a public map.
  • Visibility control: you can stop appearing in nearby discovery when you do not want to be found.
  • Permission-based DMs: a private chat begins only after the intro is accepted.
  • Block and report: either action cuts off contact; reports also give moderators something concrete to review.
  • Rate limits: limits on intros and messages make bulk harassment and automated spam harder.

These controls reduce exposure, but your choices still matter. Do not reveal your address, building, workplace, routine, or live travel path. If you decide to meet someone, use a busy public place, control your own transport, and tell a trusted person where you are going. Who Circles Is For

Circles is useful when global random chat feels too disconnected but profile-heavy social apps feel too exposed. It suits someone new to an area, an adult who wants platonic local conversation, a night-shift worker awake at unusual hours, or anyone who would rather start with words than a photograph.

It is not a tool for tracking people, finding a specific person, contacting minors, or bypassing somebody's refusal. The privacy and consent model works only when everyone respects those boundaries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Circles an Android or iPhone app?

Circles currently works as a mobile-friendly web app at chatrio.app/circles. Open it in your phone's browser; do not assume an unrelated app-store listing with a similar name is the Chatrio product.

Does Circles require an account?

No email, phone number, or password is required for the nearby-chat experience. An anonymous token on your device keeps your Circles identity consistent between visits on that browser.

Can other users see my exact location?

No. Nearby discovery uses a coarse area and rough distance labels. For the design in plain language, read how approximate location protects nearby chat.

What should my first intro say?
